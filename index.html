1. Title + Abstract

Abstract

This study investigates whether content pages can be grouped into distinct performance archetypes using search visibility, clicks, click-through rate (CTR), and their observed trends over a six-month period. Daily content-performance data were aggregated into monthly page-level metrics for the October 2025 through March 2026 observation window, and pages with performance observations in at least four of the six months were retained. Six performance features were standardized and used to evaluate a rule-based baseline against a K-Means clustering approach, with both methods evaluated on the same 30,000-page sample. The rule-based baseline produced a silhouette score of -0.027875, while the K-Means model with five clusters produced a silhouette score of 0.889132, indicating substantially stronger separation in the feature space used for evaluation. The resulting segments were translated into reproducible Protect, Improve, and Monitor recommendations intended to support content-review prioritization rather than make causal claims about search performance.

2. Introduction / Problem Statement

Large content inventories contain pages with substantially different levels and directions of search performance. Some pages generate considerable search visibility and clicks, while others have limited measurable activity or exhibit declining performance. Applying the same optimization approach to every page can make content prioritization difficult.

This project examines whether pages can be grouped according to their observed search-performance behavior using a combination of level and trend features.

Research question:
Can content pages be grouped into distinct performance archetypes using visibility, click, CTR, and trend signals over the October 2025–March 2026 observation window, and can those archetypes support reproducible content-prioritization recommendations?

The analysis is intended to support a practical content-review workflow. In particular, the resulting segments are used as evidence for deciding which pages may warrant protection, improvement, or monitoring.

The project does not attempt to establish that any particular content action caused a change in Google Search performance. The analysis is observational and should therefore be interpreted as decision support.

3. Data

3.1 Dataset and observation window

The analysis uses the FlyRank internship warehouse's daily content-performance data. The final feature-engineering analysis was restricted to:

October 2025 through March 2026

Daily page-level search-performance measurements were aggregated to monthly values before page-level features were calculated.

3.2 Monthly aggregation

For each page and month, the analysis calculated:

  • total impressions;
  • total clicks;
  • CTR, calculated as monthly clicks divided by monthly impressions when impressions were greater than zero;
  • average Google Search Console position.

3.3 Page inclusion criteria

Pages were required to have observations in at least four of the six months. After feature engineering and this coverage filter, the final dataset contained 254,802 content pages.

3.5 Trend calculation

Trend variables were calculated using an ordinary least-squares regression slope against sequential month number. A positive slope indicates an increasing direction and a negative slope indicates a decreasing direction. These slopes describe the observed direction during the six-month window and are not forecasts.

4. Methodology

4.1 Analytical approach

  1. Aggregate daily performance to monthly page-level metrics.
  2. Construct page-level performance and trend features.
  3. Compare a rule-based baseline with K-Means clustering.
  4. Translate the resulting segments into reproducible content-prioritization recommendations.

4.2 Feature standardization

The six clustering variables have substantially different numerical scales. The features were therefore standardized using StandardScaler before K-Means clustering.

4.3 Rule-based baseline

A simple rule-based segmentation was created as an interpretable baseline. The baseline classified pages using average impressions, average clicks, average CTR, and their observed trends. Performance thresholds were based on empirical quantiles of the evaluation sample, and performance and trend categories were combined into rule-based archetypes.

The baseline was used as a simple reference method rather than as a ground-truth labeling system.

4.4 K-Means clustering

K-Means was selected because the objective was to identify groups of pages with similar multivariate performance characteristics without requiring predefined labels.

The final model used K = 5. The five-cluster solution provided a compact segmentation with strong separation in the evaluated feature space.

4.5 Baseline versus model validation

Because the dataset did not contain an independently verified archetype label, conventional supervised metrics such as accuracy, precision, recall, and F1-score were not appropriate. Both methods were instead evaluated on the same 30,000-page evaluation sample and the same six-feature standardized representation.

4.6 Leakage considerations

The clustering features were derived from performance observations within the October 2025–March 2026 analysis window. The model does not use future performance beyond this window as a prediction target, and it does not use a post-treatment outcome to determine whether an optimization action succeeded.

5.2 Cluster characteristics

Cluster 0 — broad low-signal population. Cluster 0 contains approximately 82.5% of all pages. Its average performance is relatively low compared with the high-visibility segment, and its average CTR and trend values are comparatively modest. Because it contains most pages, it should not be interpreted as a homogeneous business category; it represents the broad majority of pages without the distinctive high-volume or high-CTR patterns found in smaller clusters.

Cluster 1 — high-visibility performance segment. Cluster 1 contains 1,203 pages. Its average monthly impressions and clicks are substantially higher than those of the other groups, with approximately 19,981 average monthly impressions, 78.34 average monthly clicks, and 0.00435 average CTR. Its average impression and click trends are strongly positive. These pages represent a comparatively high-visibility segment that may warrant closer review before significant content changes.

Cluster 2 — moderate/high visibility segment. Cluster 2 contains 41,467 pages. Its average monthly impressions are substantially above the broad Cluster 0 population but below Cluster 1. The group also exhibits positive average impression and click trends, representing pages with meaningful search visibility and measurable activity.

Clusters 3 and 4 — high-CTR, low-volume segments. Clusters 3 and 4 are small groups characterized by relatively low absolute search volume but unusually high CTR values compared with the broader population. They also have distinct trend profiles, providing a reason to retain them as separate data-driven segments. Because these groups are small, their characteristics should be interpreted cautiously.

6. Limitations & Honest Framing

6.1 No external ground-truth archetype labels

The dataset does not contain an independently verified label identifying the correct performance archetype for each page. The clustering is therefore an unsupervised segmentation, not a supervised classification problem.

6.2 Observational analysis

The analysis describes associations and observed performance patterns. It cannot establish that a particular content optimization, update, rewrite, or other intervention caused an increase or decrease in Google Search performance.

6.3 Six-month observation window

The analysis covers October 2025 through March 2026. Patterns observed during this period may not remain stable indefinitely. Seasonality, changes in search demand, content changes, technical changes, and other external factors may influence future performance.

6.4 Highly skewed performance distributions

Performance variables are highly skewed. Many pages have very low or zero observed impressions and clicks, while a small number have very high volumes. Standardization makes the features comparable in scale but does not eliminate the underlying skew.

A future robustness analysis could test transformations such as log1p and compare whether the resulting segmentation remains stable. That robustness analysis was not part of the final model.

6.5 Uneven cluster sizes

The final clustering contains one very large group and several small groups. This may reflect genuine structure in the content population, but the smaller clusters should be interpreted cautiously.

6.6 Recommendations are rule-derived

Protect, Improve, and Monitor are not independent labels supplied by the dataset. They are decision rules constructed from observed performance and trend features. They should therefore be treated as a reproducible prioritization framework rather than ground-truth classifications.

7. Ranked Recommendations

The clustering was translated into three operational queues. These queues are intended to organize review attention rather than predict business outcomes.

ActionPagesShareInterpretation
Protect 79 0.03% High-visibility pages with positive impression and click signals and sufficient CTR.
Improve 33,043 12.97% Pages with meaningful visibility plus at least one signal warranting investigation.
Monitor 221,680 87.00% Pages without sufficient signal under the selected rules for immediate prioritization.

7.1 Protect

The Protect queue contains pages meeting the highest-visibility and positive-performance conditions in the action rules. The rule requires at least 30,000 average monthly impressions, positive impression trend, CTR of at least 0.004, and a non-negative click trend.

These pages can be treated as high-visibility assets where unnecessary changes deserve additional scrutiny. Protect does not mean that these pages should never be changed.

7.2 Improve

The Improve queue identifies pages with meaningful visibility but at least one performance signal that warrants investigation, including declining impressions, declining clicks, low CTR despite meaningful visibility, or growth accompanied by an improvement opportunity.

Follow-up review may consider search-intent alignment, title and snippet relevance, content completeness, internal linking, content freshness, competing search results, and technical/indexing conditions. These are investigation areas, not claims that any individual factor caused the observed performance.

7.3 Monitor

The Monitor queue contains pages that do not meet the stronger conditions for Protect or Improve. Its large size indicates that most pages in the analyzed population do not produce sufficient signal under the selected rules to justify immediate prioritization.

7.4 Priority ordering

Within the action queues, a priority score was used to create a review order. The score combines log-transformed visibility, the magnitude of impression and click trends, and an action weighting. Its purpose is review sequencing, not measurement of business value.
